EchoVitals

A radar-based platform for contactless vital health sign sensing.

 Heart rate and breathing monitoring in settings where wearable contact-based sensors are impractical.

Arman Israfil1,  Sk Sahil Ahamed1,  Md Qais Khan1,  Md Faizan1

1 Dept. of Electronics and Communication Engineering

Aliah University

Kolkata, India

Abstract: EchoVitals is a low-cost, contactless platform that measures heart rate and breathing rate by reflecting a 60 GHz frequency-modulated continuous-wave (FMCW) radar signal off the chest wall, with no electrodes, straps, or wires. Contact-based sensors are impractical for burn and infectious patients, infants, and high-stress operators, and are uncomfortable for long-term wear (Fig. 1). An integrated FMCW radar module performs on-chip range-gating and phase extraction to separate the heartbeat's sub-millimetre motion from the much larger motion of breathing, and a lightweight host-side layer validates and smooths the resulting readings. We target accuracy comparable to the module's rated performance across the deployment settings in Fig. 2.

Index Terms : contactless vital-sign monitoring, 60 GHz FMCW radar, mmWave sensing, heart rate, respiration rate.

I. Introduction

A. Problem statement & why and where contact-based devices fail :Continuous contact sensing such as ECG pads, chest straps, and clip-on pulse oximeters  requires wiring. Also, for adhesive types, skin contact is impractical in case of burn and infectious patients, easily irritated for neonates(infants), and restrictive for drivers and pilots. Fig. 1 summarises the recurring failure modes this creates.

Fig. 1. Recurring failure modes of contact-based vital sign sensors.

B. Proposed solution:  EchoVitals senses the chest wall directly using a compact radar-on-module: a self-contained transceiver and antenna array paired with an onboard digital signal processor that runs the full frequency-modulated continuous-wave (FMCW) ranging and vital-sign extraction pipeline in hardware. The short wavelength at this frequency band gives the module's phase-based ranging technique the sensitivity needed to resolve the chest wall's sub-millimetre cardiac displacement beneath its much larger respiratory motion, with no external amplification stage. The module reports breathing rate, heart rate, target range, and target count over a standard serial link; the ESP32 microcontroller forwards these values to a host script for validation, smoothing, and display.

C. Deployment settings:  This applies wherever continuous contact sensing is impractical: infants and NICU care, burn and infectious-disease wards, drivers, pilots and train operators, elderly bed and bathroom monitoring, smart mattresses for sleep tracking, and fitness or gym use i.e mapped in Fig. 2. Longer-range scenarios such as search-and-rescue remain future work: the module's rated sensing range (0.4–1.5 m) is built for a fixed, near-body mount, not a moving drone.

III. Methodology

The sensing stage is built around a single-chip 60 GHz FMCW radar transceiver. The module emits a sequence of linear frequency chirps and mixes each received echo with the transmitted chirp to produce an intermediate frequency beat signal whose frequency encodes target range. A range-FFT across each chirp resolves the subject's chest wall as a single range bin, and the phase of that bin is tracked chirp-to-chirp to recover sub-millimetre surface displacement,  the basis of micro-Doppler vital-sign sensing. Onboard firmware separates this phase history into two frequency bands: a low-frequency, high-amplitude component corresponding to respiration, and a higher-frequency, low-amplitude component corresponding to the heartbeat, and reports both as periodic rate values together with a target count and range estimate.

On the host side, EchoVitals treats these periodic values as a lightweight telemetry stream rather than a raw waveform. A parsing layer reads each frame; a validity gate cross-checks the reported target count and range against the expected monitoring zone and discards frames where no subject is present or the subject has left the sensing envelope; and a short moving-average stage smooths the surviving readings before they reach the display. The signal-processing burden , chirp generation, range-gating, and respiration/cardiac band separation is carried entirely by the radar module's onboard DSP, keeping the host implementation small and auditable. IV. Desired Output and Expected Results

A working prototype displaying live heart rate and breathing rate on a bedside dashboard, mounted 0.4–1.5 m from a seated or lying subject. We will validate against a reference pulse oximeter for heart rate and a manual 60-second breath count for breathing rate, across at least four consenting team volunteers, and record the module's own reported target confidence alongside each reading. Manufacturer-published figures for this class of module report respiratory accuracy around 90% and cardiac accuracy in the 85–95% range depending on test conditions; we report our own measured error rather than assuming a datasheet figure holds in our setup.

V. Conclusion

EchoVitals shows that reliable, contact-free vital-sign monitoring is achievable with a single integrated 60 GHz FMCW radar module, a microcontroller, and a lightweight validation layer  without the fragile analog front end or complex filtering pipeline that raw-radar approaches typically require. The system measures heart rate and breathing directly from chest-wall motion, targets accuracy comparable to its sensor class through reference-validated testing and is designed for deployment across the patient groups and environments outlined in this proposal. It offers a practical, low-cost foundation for non-contact patient monitoring that can be extended toward broader clinical and field use.
